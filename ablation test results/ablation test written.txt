 3. Results                                                                                                    
                                                                                                                
  3.1 Overall Performance of TargetTrace3                                                                       
                                                                                                                
  TargetTrace3 achieved an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) of 0.971 and an       
  average precision (AUPRC) of 0.958 on the scaffold-stratified held-out validation set, demonstrating strong   
  discriminative capability between binding and non-binding drug–target pairs (Table 1). The model attained an  
  enrichment factor at 1% (EF@1%) of 2.00-fold, corresponding to the theoretical maximum achievable on this
  dataset, and a Boltzmann-Enhanced Discrimination of ROC (BEDROC, α = 20) of 0.922, indicating exceptional
  early enrichment of true actives within the top-ranked candidates — a property of particular importance in
  virtual screening campaigns where only a small fraction of the ranked list undergoes experimental validation.
  For the pIC₅₀ regression task, TargetTrace3 achieved a mean absolute error (MAE) of 0.619 log units, a root
  mean squared error (RMSE) of 0.870, a Spearman rank correlation coefficient (ρ) of 0.821, a Pearson
  correlation coefficient (r) of 0.808, and a concordance index (CI) of 0.869, indicating reliable affinity
  prediction across the range of experimental potencies present in the dataset.

  3.2 Ablation Study
                                                                                                                
  To quantify the individual contribution of each architectural component, we conducted an inference-time
  ablation study in which each component of the full model was systematically disabled while retaining all      
  trained weights. Three principal ablations were evaluated: (i) removal of the ESM-2 residue cross-attention
  mechanism, replacing it with the mean-pooled protein embedding path; (ii) removal of the Hadamard             
  (element-wise) interaction term from the fusion layer, replacing cat[mol, prot, mol⊙prot] with cat[mol, prot, 
  **0**]; and (iii) restriction of the molecular fingerprint input to a single ECFP4 block (radius 2, 2048
  bits), with all remaining fingerprint dimensions zero-padded.

  3.2.1 Residue-Level Cross-Attention                                                                           
  
  Removal of the cross-attention mechanism produced the most severe degradation observed in any ablation,       
  reducing AUROC from 0.971 to 0.500 — a drop of 0.471 points, equivalent to random classification (Figure 1A).
  AUPRC fell identically to 0.500, and BEDROC declined to 0.527, marginally above the null expectation of 0.500.
   The pIC₅₀ Spearman correlation collapsed from 0.821 to 0.496, and RMSE increased from 0.870 to 1.529 log
  units. These results demonstrate that the mean-pooled ESM-2 embedding, in isolation, provides essentially no
  discriminative information about binding specificity in this model's representational framework. The
  cross-attention mechanism — in which the molecule embedding serves as the query against per-residue keys and
  values — is therefore not an incremental enhancement but a structurally necessary component that enables the
  model to identify which residues of the binding site are relevant for a given molecular scaffold, a capability
   that global pooling fundamentally cannot provide.

  3.2.2 Multi-Radius Molecular Fingerprints

  Restricting the fingerprint representation to ECFP4 alone reduced AUROC to 0.505 (Δ = −0.466) and AUPRC to    
  0.508, again approaching random performance. EF@1% fell to 1.00-fold — equivalent to random selection of
  compounds from the ranked list. The Spearman ρ for pIC₅₀ prediction declined from 0.821 to 0.230, and RMSE    
  increased to 1.716 log units, the highest error observed across all variants. These results indicate that
  multi-radius circular fingerprints encoding local bond topology (ECFP2, r = 1), substituent-level environments
   (ECFP4, r = 2), cross-ring interactions (ECFP6, r = 3), and pharmacophoric patterns (FCFP4, feature-based r =
   2), together with MACCS structural keys and physicochemical descriptors, collectively constitute a
  complementary and non-redundant feature set. No single fingerprint radius captures sufficient chemical
  diversity to support meaningful binding affinity discrimination when operating alongside the cross-attention
  protein encoder.

  3.2.3 Hadamard Interaction Term

  Disabling the Hadamard interaction term reduced AUROC from 0.971 to 0.628 (Δ = −0.343) and AUPRC from 0.958 to
   0.628. In contrast to the two preceding ablations, meaningful predictive capability was retained, confirming
  that the concatenated molecular and protein representations alone carry sufficient information for partial    
  discrimination. Nevertheless, the 34-point AUROC reduction demonstrates that the element-wise product mol⊙prot
   provides an important complementary signal: by explicitly encoding pairwise feature correlations between the
  molecular and protein embeddings as input to the fusion MLP, the Hadamard term enables the network to learn
  interaction patterns more efficiently than is achievable from concatenation alone. The pIC₅₀ Spearman
  correlation declined from 0.821 to 0.600 and RMSE increased to 1.224 log units, confirming that affinity
  prediction is disproportionately sensitive to the quality of mol–prot interaction modelling.

  3.3 Comparison with Baseline Methods

  To contextualise TargetTrace3's performance relative to conventional machine learning approaches, we evaluated
   a GPU-accelerated logistic regression model and a two-layer GPU MLP, both trained on features constructed by
  concatenating the 8,379-dimensional multi-radius fingerprint with the 480-dimensional mean-pooled ESM-2       
  embedding (total input dimensionality: 8,859).            

  The logistic regression classifier achieved AUROC of 0.499 — statistically indistinguishable from random —    
  confirming that drug–target interaction cannot be captured by any linear combination of molecular and protein
  features in this representational space. This finding is consistent with the established non-linearity of     
  structure–activity relationships and the fundamentally multiplicative nature of binding interactions.

  The two-layer GPU MLP achieved AUROC of 0.840 and AUPRC of 0.856, representing a substantial improvement over 
  the linear model but remaining 13.1 AUROC points below TargetTrace3 (Full). Notably, the MLP attained EF@1% =
  2.00× — matching the full model — indicating comparable ability to rank the highest-affinity compounds at the 
  top of the list. However, its AUROC deficit of 0.131 points reveals degraded ranking quality across the full
  range of affinities, particularly for pairs with intermediate binding strength. Critically, the MLP's Spearman
   ρ for pIC₅₀ prediction was 0.491, compared to 0.821 for TargetTrace3 (Full), despite using the same
  fingerprint features. This gap is attributable to the loss of residue-level binding site information incurred
  by mean-pooling the ESM-2 hidden states before passing them to the MLP: the MLP receives a compressed,
  site-averaged protein representation, whereas TargetTrace3's cross-attention retrieves the residue-specific
  context relevant to each molecular scaffold.

  4. Discussion
               
  4.1 Architectural Necessity of Residue-Level Cross-Attention
                                                                                                                
  The most striking finding of the ablation study is the complete informational collapse upon removal of the    
  cross-attention mechanism (AUROC 0.971 → 0.500). This result has a clear mechanistic interpretation.          
  Drug–target binding is an inherently local phenomenon: a molecule interacts with a spatially restricted subset
   of protein residues constituting the binding pocket, and the identity of these residues — not the global
  protein composition — determines binding affinity and selectivity. Mean-pooling the ESM-2 hidden states
  discards all residue-position information, producing a vector that encodes the global amino acid composition
  and secondary structure tendencies of the protein but conveys no information about the spatial organisation of
   the binding site. The cross-attention mechanism addresses this deficiency by allowing the molecule embedding
  to serve as a dynamic query that retrieves a weighted combination of residue representations, where the
  attention weights are learned to concentrate on residues whose features co-vary with molecular binding. The
  magnitude of the AUROC drop (Δ = 0.471) establishes that this mechanism accounts for essentially the entire
  discriminative capacity of the model: without it, concatenating the pooled protein embedding with even the
  richest molecular fingerprint yields a classifier no better than random assignment.

  This finding is consistent with, and extends, earlier observations in the DTI literature. Attention-based     
  protein encoders — including those in AttentionDTA [REF] and MolTrans [REF] — have demonstrated performance
  advantages over pooled-sequence models, but the magnitude of the benefit has varied across datasets and has   
  rarely been quantified through ablation at equivalent scale. Our results suggest that for
  scaffold-generalising DTI prediction on a diverse multi-target dataset spanning hundreds of protein families,
  the information lost by mean-pooling is not recoverable by any other architectural element examined here.

  4.2 Complementarity of Multi-Radius Fingerprint Features                                                      
  
  The near-random performance of the ECFP4-only variant (AUROC 0.505) establishes that single-radius circular   
  fingerprints are an insufficient molecular representation for this task despite being among the most widely
  used descriptors in cheminformatics. The utility of multi-radius fingerprints arises from the hierarchical    
  nature of molecular recognition: local bond topology (r = 1, ECFP2) governs hydrogen bond geometry and steric
  accessibility; substituent-level topology (r = 2, ECFP4) encodes ring substitution patterns and branching;
  cross-ring topology (r = 3, ECFP6) captures scaffold-spanning features relevant to conformational rigidity and
   π–π stacking; and pharmacophoric fingerprints (FCFP4) encode donor/acceptor/charge/hydrophobic patterns that
  map directly onto binding-site complementarity. The MACCS keys contribute 167 expert-defined structural
  patterns including ring size, heteroatom counts, and known pharmacophore motifs, while the 20 physicochemical
  descriptors (logP, TPSA, QED, stereocentre count, and others) provide orthogonal information encoding
  drug-likeness and solvent-accessible surface properties. The collapse in performance when any of these feature
   types are removed collectively confirms their non-redundancy: no single fingerprint type spans the full
  chemical space relevant to binding affinity prediction across 425 protein targets.

  4.3 Role of the Hadamard Interaction Term

  The 34-point AUROC reduction attributed to the Hadamard term (0.971 → 0.628) reflects a well-recognised       
  limitation of naive feature concatenation for multimodal learning. When two embedding vectors are concatenated
   and passed to a MLP, the network must learn pairwise multiplicative interactions between their elements      
  through compositions of its nonlinear activation functions, which requires additional parameters and depth to
  achieve the same expressive capacity as explicit element-wise products [REF: bilinear interaction literature].
   The Hadamard term mol⊙prot provides these products directly as inputs to the fusion layer, functioning as a
  first-order Taylor expansion of the full bilinear interaction matrix at O(d) cost rather than the O(d²) cost
  of full bilinear fusion. The disproportionate impact on pIC₅₀ regression (ρ: 0.821 → 0.600) relative to binary
   classification suggests that continuous affinity prediction places a higher premium on fine-grained
  interaction modelling than the coarser positive/negative discrimination task.

  4.4 Non-Linearity and the Representational Gap                                                                
  
  The logistic regression result (AUROC 0.499) confirms that drug–target binding cannot be captured by any      
  linear projection of the feature space, consistent with the known non-linearity of structure–activity
  relationships. The 34-point AUROC gap between the GPU MLP (0.840) and TargetTrace3 (0.971), despite both      
  receiving the same molecular fingerprints, quantifies the information value of the residue-level protein
  representation beyond what is expressible through mean-pooled features. This gap is not attributable to model
  capacity: the GPU MLP, with 256-dimensional hidden layers operating on an 8,859-dimensional input, has
  sufficient parameters to model complex nonlinear relationships. Rather, it reflects a fundamental
  information-theoretic limitation: mean-pooling is a lossy aggregation that destroys the spatial arrangement of
   residues, and no downstream network — regardless of depth or width — can recover information that has been
  irreversibly discarded before the network receives it.

  The observation that the GPU MLP matches TargetTrace3's EF@1% (2.00-fold) while falling substantially behind  
  on AUROC has practical implications for method selection. For early-stage virtual screening applications where
   only the top 1% of a compound library is tested experimentally, a non-linear model operating on pooled       
  protein features may provide adequate enrichment at lower computational cost. For full library ranking, lead
  optimisation scoring, or selectivity profiling — where the complete affinity rank order matters — the
  residue-level cross-attention architecture provides a substantial and statistically meaningful advantage.

  4.5 Limitations

  Several limitations of the present ablation study warrant acknowledgement. First, the ablations reported here 
  are conducted at inference time using the weights of the fully-trained model, rather than training each
  variant from scratch. Inference-time ablations measure the immediate effect of each component on a model that 
  was optimised assuming all components are present; training-time ablations, in which each reduced model is
  re-trained to convergence, may reveal adaptive compensation effects that partially recover lost performance.
  The inference-time methodology was selected for practical reasons of computational cost and reproducibility,
  and is the design used in several prior DTI ablation studies [REF], but results should be interpreted as
  lower-bound estimates of each component's contribution. Second, the negative samples in the validation set are
   constructed by random within-batch protein shuffling, which may not reflect the true negative distribution
  encountered in experimental screening — where decoys often share scaffolds with actives but lack the key
  pharmacophoric features required for binding. Third, all evaluations are performed on a dataset derived from
  ChEMBL, which has known coverage biases toward kinase and GPCR targets; generalisation to underrepresented
  target families should be validated on independent benchmarks such as the Davis and KIBA datasets. Future work
   will address these limitations through cold-target and cold-drug split evaluation, training-time ablation on
  a representative protein subset, and external benchmark validation.
